# Product Review Report

## Executive summary

Product performance is moving in the right direction. From the June 18 to August 12 releases, change failure rate fell from 4.2% to 2.4%, median task time improved from 6.8 to 5.5 minutes, and user satisfaction rose from 4.1 to 4.5. Customer feedback reinforces the value of recent investments in approvals, live progress, and task recovery.

The clearest remaining gaps are understanding which workspace is active, producing more consistently structured research outputs, and finding or downloading completed reports. These needs support the Q3 focus on controlled workspaces and research brief templates, while providing evidence to prioritize reusable reporting templates and improved artifact previews among the Q4 candidates.

## Three most important feedback themes

### 1. Workspace context needs to be unmistakable

Operations users report that workspace names do not clearly identify the local folder in use. This is a high-priority trust and safety issue because ambiguity about execution context can lead to mistakes even when approval controls work as intended. It directly supports the in-progress Q3 controlled-workspaces initiative.

### 2. Visibility builds confidence throughout task execution

Users value seeing exact commands before approving an action and following long-running work in the live activity timeline. Positive feedback on task availability after reconnecting adds to the same broader need: users want work to remain understandable and recoverable from approval through completion. These strengths should be preserved as workflows evolve.

### 3. Outputs need stronger structure and discoverability

Research briefs need a consistent place for assumptions and unresolved questions, while finished reports need to be easier to locate and download. Together, these comments point to an end-to-end output problem: artifacts must be both decision-ready and easy to retrieve. This theme supports the Q3 research brief templates and the Q4 candidates for reusable reporting templates, export, and artifact previews.

## Release performance observations

- Change failure rate improved in every release, declining 1.8 percentage points overall, from 4.2% to 2.4% (about a 43% relative reduction).
- Median task time fell by 1.3 minutes overall, from 6.8 to 5.5 minutes (about a 19% improvement). Dynamic approvals briefly increased the median from 5.9 to 6.1 minutes, but the subsequent live activity timeline release reduced it to the best result in the series.
- User satisfaction increased with every release, rising from 4.1 to 4.5. The latest release combined the highest satisfaction, lowest failure rate, and fastest median task time.
- Qualitative feedback aligns with the release data: exact approval commands, the live activity timeline, and post-reconnect task recovery all received positive comments.
- The dataset contains only four releases over roughly two months, so the direction is encouraging but should be validated with more releases and usage-volume context before attributing improvements to individual features.

## Recommended next actions

1. **Finish workspace-context improvements in Q3.** Show the active workspace name and local folder prominently at task start, during approval, and in the activity view. Validate the design with Operations users and track workspace-selection errors or clarification requests.
2. **Standardize research brief outputs.** Use the discovery work to define a template with evidence, assumptions, unresolved questions, and source-quality cues. Pilot it with Product users before broader rollout.
3. **Prioritize artifact retrieval in Q4 planning.** Move improved export and artifact previews ahead using the negative Leadership feedback as supporting evidence. Provide a clear completion destination with find, preview, and download actions.
4. **Protect successful visibility and recovery behaviors.** Treat exact approval commands, live progress, and reconnect recovery as acceptance criteria for future workflow changes; monitor their satisfaction signals for regressions.
5. **Strengthen release measurement.** Continue tracking failure rate, task time, and satisfaction, but add task volume, segment-level results, workspace-context errors, artifact-download success, and feature adoption. Review dynamic approvals specifically to determine whether the temporary task-time increase reflects necessary review time or avoidable friction.
